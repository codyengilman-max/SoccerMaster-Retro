# SoccerMaster — Adaptive Story Engine
## Version 0.2 · 15 October 2026

Implementation specification and authored opening chapter. Not an implemented or playtested game engine. Proposed numerical settings below are product tuning choices, not research findings. Target repository: codyengilman-max/SoccerMaster-Retro; retain its TypeScript/Vite/Phaser architecture, existing saves, growth systems, tactical moments and current gameplay repairs.

## 1. Player promise

Become a distinct young soccer player whose actions are remembered. Performance, development, relationships and opportunities are separate. Success can introduce responsibility or conflict; failure can open a learning path. Neither automatically causes punishment or reward.

The first chapter spans four game weeks and works for every supported position, including goalkeeper. It does not require league/tournament implementation. Use existing supported matches or explicitly identified exhibition fixtures. Never fabricate league standings.

## 2. Identity contract

At career creation choose Boy or Girl, enter a player name, select an available appearance and supported primary position. Do this before any named dialogue, commentary or journal entry. A short neutral visual intro may precede setup. This supersedes the earlier proposal to defer full identity setup until after the cold open.

Canonical identity:

    playerId: immutable generated ID
    displayName: chosen Unicode name
    genderChoice: boy | girl
    appearanceId: approved asset ID
    primaryRole: existing game role ID
    identityRevision: integer

Derive pronouns from genderChoice:
- boy: he / him / his (adjective) / his (standalone) / himself
- girl: she / her / her (adjective) / hers (standalone) / herself

Use tokens:
- {{player.name}}, {{player.subject}}, {{player.object}}
- {{player.possessiveAdjective}}, {{player.possessivePronoun}}
- {{player.reflexive}}, {{player.Subject}}
- {{role.label}}, {{coach.name}}, {{rival.name}}, {{sam.name}}, {{jordan.name}}, {{caregiver.label}}

Example:
Coach: “{{player.name}}, show me what you noticed.”
Sam: “Give {{player.object}} a second. {{player.Subject}} just got here.”
Journal: “{{player.name}} completed the first week.”

Direct dialogue normally uses “you.” Prefer “the place for {{player.name}}” over constructing English possessives from arbitrary names. Gender choice must not change attributes, difficulty, opportunities, available positions, story worth or reward rules. Appearance does not determine ability.

Gender choice does not automatically assign a competition category or switch clubs; use the supported career/team context. Any competition-policy design is a separate requirement.

Display-name rules: trim external whitespace; normalize NFC; accept international names, spaces, apostrophes and hyphens; require 1–24 grapheme clusters, at most 96 Unicode code points and 384 UTF-8 bytes after NFC normalization; reject malformed Unicode, explicit bidi formatting/override/isolate controls (not ordinary right-to-left letters), and more than eight consecutive combining marks; preserve legitimate script joiners; reject line breaks, control characters and reserved template delimiters. Explain validation errors. Do not require a legal name or send names to external services.

Render text as text, never HTML. No eval or arbitrary property traversal. Resolve only allowlisted tokens in authored templates in a single pass; never reinterpret substituted player text. Missing required tokens fail content validation.

All surfaces use canonical identity: dialogue, profile, lineup, HUD when named, objectives, journal, rewards, end-of-match feedback, save labels and chapter endings. Store journal template IDs and structured arguments, not baked-in player-name sentences. Cosmetic renaming updates rendered references without changing playerId, event ownership or rewards. NPCs with the same display name remain distinct by ID.

Existing careers missing identity get a resume-safe setup step, not a guessed gender. Keep prior progress and IDs. Do not regenerate match rewards during migration.

## 3. Cast bible

These are fictional working characters. Preserve player gender choice independently from the supporting cast.

COACH — Coach Code; he/him. Wants intelligent, improving football. Direct, occasionally dry, sometimes explains a decision too late. Must acknowledge a misunderstanding when appropriate. Never humiliates the player.
Voice: “Good finish. Now tell me what you saw before it.”

RIVAL — Alex; they/them. Wants a meaningful role. Competitive, observant, privately worries about becoming replaceable. Concern is shown through behavior before personal disclosure. High rivalry and high respect can coexist.
Voice: “I was making that run. You saw it, right?”

TEAMMATE — Sam; they/them. Wants belonging and increasing independence. Funny, perceptive, reluctant to speak up. Has agency: can decline help, improve, or disagree.
Voice: “I had a plan. The ball had another plan.”

SCHOOL_FRIEND — Jordan; they/them. Wants a friendship that extends beyond soccer. Introduced in week two. Can be curious about soccer without becoming the player's motivational assistant.
Voice: “You said five minutes. Was that normal time or football time?”

CAREGIVER — selectable neutral label: Parent, Mom, Dad, or Guardian. Use direct speech and label without inferring family structure. Wants to support the player, sometimes offers advice too quickly.
Voice: “Do you want ideas, encouragement, or a quiet ride?”

Prototype relationship values: trust/respect start 50, rivalry 20; clamp each to 0–100. Ordinary scene changes at most 3 points, major reconciliations at most 5. Values are development tuning, not labels shown as a moral score. A scene cannot repeatedly farm points.

No global goodness score. No permanent personality label from one answer. No story-authored technical-stat penalties.

## 5. Evidence adapter

Consume immutable events emitted by authoritative game systems:
matchCompleted, playerActionResolved, objectiveEvaluated, weeklyFocusSelected,
trainingActivityCompleted, homePracticeLogged, roleOpportunityRecorded.

Each event carries unique eventId, stable producer source-record ID, careerId, chapterRunId, match/session ID where relevant, gameWeek, phaseSlotId, actorId, roleAtEvent, teamAtEvent, event type, schema version and minimal payload. The adapter validates IDs, bounds and ownership.

Evidence distinctions:
- Observed: confirmed score, minutes, recorded action.
- Evaluated: tactical assessment with evaluator/version reference.
- Self-reported: home-practice log; never proof of mastery.
- Unknown: absent evidence; cannot support factual allegations.

Do not infer “selfish,” “lazy,” “ignored an open teammate,” or “excellent positioning” from score or pass count alone. Without suitable evaluation evidence, ask a neutral question or select a different scene.

Role-specific evidence: attack (finishing/creation/support), midfield (receiving/options/progression), defence (cover/interceptions/build-up), goalkeeper (positioning/distribution/saves). Only use measurements already supported or implement explicit tested adapters. No outfield-goal requirement for goalkeeper advancement.

## 6. Story director

Boundary scheduling only: pre-training, post-training, pre-match, post-match, home/reflection. Never interrupt an active touch/shot for ordinary story dialogue.

1. Ingest and deduplicate authoritative events.
2. Resolve validated completions once; offer explicit confirmation before advancing past a due commitment. Expire unaccepted invitations harmlessly.
3. Filter scene candidates by phase, evidence, role and existing conflict.
4. Exclude invalid asset references, missing tokens, contradictions and cooldown violations.
5. Prioritize due follow-ups, then unresolved conflicts, weekly focus and unused character beats.
6. Select among equal-priority valid alternatives using saved seeded state.
7. Persist the selected instance before displaying it. Reload must restore it, not reroll it.
8. Present choices, commit one valid selection and effects together, then schedule eligible follow-ups.

Priority ties use stable scene ID order before seeded selection. Default maximum: one major conflict and one minor relationship thread. Unused events need not become story scenes. No forced negative-success percentage.

One required pre-match scene and one required post-match scene at most; optional scenes between sessions. Aim for 20–45 seconds of reading per required scene, no response timer. This is a tuning target. Existing 4–6 Coach Moments remain the total match learning interruptions.

Story may weight eligible Coach Moment topics toward weekly focus; never force an impossible scenario or change a correct answer to suit the plot.

At four game weeks, an unresolved chapter conflict receives an honest reflection/resolution scene. Missing matches offer resume or an explicit skip path. A skipped match remains unobserved; never invent a result. No wall-clock absence penalties.

## 7. Conflicts

C_ROLE covers competition for a supported role opportunity. C_ADAPT covers ambition versus a supported team task. They share one major-conflict slot, with explicit pause/resume. C_PROMISE covers an explicitly confirmed in-game commitment and occupies the minor-thread slot. No real-world training obligation can incur a story penalty. The complete authoritative transition table is section 14; scene summaries never add implicit transitions.

## 8. Authored chapter: The Shirt Isn't Yours Yet

Each numbered row is a unique scene template. Choices shown after A/B/C have the effects specified; all unstated state remains unchanged. All relationship effects below are proposed bounded values. No choice directly changes technical attributes.

### Week 1 — The New Kid

S01 identity setup / bedroom.
Visual: boots, ball, empty career journal.
Prompt: “What should we call you?”
Action: complete identity, then choose ambition: starting opportunity / learning a role / dependable contribution.
Effect: save ambition; no stat bonus, no hidden difficulty change. Continue to S02.

S02 first practice / pitch.
Coach: “{{player.name}}, join this group. Play what you see.”
Action: short real practice sequence tailored to role; Alex participates where possible.
Effect: save actual practice evidence. Completion or explicit skip proceeds to S03; retry remains in S02 until completion or skip. Skipped practice has no fabricated performance.

S03 whistle / pitch edge.
If confirmed successful combination with Alex: Alex says “That worked. Again?”
If confirmed attempted combination failed: Alex says “We nearly had that.”
Otherwise: Alex says “First session. A lot to take in.”
A “Let's try another.” → interest in combination practice.
B “What did you see?” → unlock optional view of recorded situation, or neutral explanation if none.
C “I'm still finding my feet.” → Sam offers brief encouragement.
No moral ranking or automatic relationship points. Save the selected follow-up: combination invitation, evidence review, or Sam encouragement. An invitation alone creates no obligation.

S04 water bottles / training ground.
Sam offers an awkward fist bump while the player reaches for water.
Sam: “Perfect coordination. We're going to be brilliant.”
A laugh together → Sam trust +2.
B introduce yourself quietly → Sam respect +2.
C “Give me a minute, then I'll catch up.” → set optional conversation invitation, no penalty.
At the end Coach identifies the week-four exhibition against a familiar opponent as the chapter destination. If a real fixture cannot be scheduled, label it a future practice match explicitly.

S05 weekly focus / home.
Caregiver: “Ideas, encouragement, or a quiet ride next time?”
A ideas / B encouragement / C quiet → save conversation preference; no relationship penalty.
Initialize the week-one focus through the focus service in section 13; choose one eligible existing weekly focus. Role-aware receiving/control is the suggested theme, not a forced selection.
Show only eligible, reviewed home practice. Missing approved content shows a clear unavailable state; story still progresses.

S06 first-match reflection / pitch exit.
Observed result is displayed accurately.
Coach: “{{player.name}}, what would you like to look at?”
A a supported positive example / B a supported difficult example / C next week's objective.
If the requested evidence is absent, explain and offer a neutral next-step discussion.
Save reflection choice. Existing growth is referenced, never awarded again.
Journal: first appearance only if an appearance occurred; otherwise first week.

### Week 2 — Same Position, Different Players

S07 role board / training.
Coach: “You and {{rival.name}} bring different things. I want to see both.”
Introduce C_ROLE.
A ask criteria → show supported role criteria; no bonus.
B name personal ambition → save coach discussion fact.
C request a demonstration → launch optional supported role challenge.
All paths lead to the same transparent evaluation criteria.

S08 Alex stays late / training exit.
Visual: Alex glances at the role board, then returns a ball to the bag.
Alex: “One more combination?”
A agree → propose a game-week-three in-game session; explicitly confirm its slot to create the commitment. Participation is optional before confirmation.
B suggest a different session → choose a supported game-time slot; commitment only after confirmation.
C decline politely → no commitment, no trust loss.
Never turn an unaccepted offer into a broken promise.

S09 Jordan / school.
Jordan: “Do I need an appointment to talk to you now?”
A tell a funny training detail → Jordan trust +2.
B ask about Jordan's day → Jordan trust +2.
C explain you are distracted and arrange a later chat → create optional invitation, not a second active conflict.
Jordan's follow-up depends on actual chosen topic. No technical rewards.

S10 practice comparison / pitch.
Play role-specific challenge with fixed transparent rules; record player and Alex results if supported.
If comparative results are unavailable, use individual feedback without claiming who won.
Coach acknowledges a specific measured strength and one next step.
A repeat in practice mode / B view feedback / C continue.
Practice replay cannot duplicate story or progression rewards.

S11 second-match aftermath / pitch exit.
Choose one evidence-supported branch:
strong role performance → responsibility conversation;
mixed performance → specific objective;
limited evidence → “I need to see more before drawing conclusions.”
Player may ask about their role, Alex's contribution, or next focus.
Strong performance can activate C_ADAPT as the major thread only after C_ROLE is resolved or explicitly paused. One active-major limit.

S12 personal reflection / home.
Show this week's actual focus and journal entries.
A retain next-week draft focus / B choose another eligible draft focus / C review then return to A/B. This edits week three, never the locked week-two focus.
Changing focus does not erase prior learning. Close the week after a valid choice.

### Week 3 — What You Promised

S13 commitment check / training.
If C_PROMISE is accepted, due or renegotiated and its agreed slot has opened, present the bound session alongside an optional individual challenge.
A keep session → launch the uniquely bound joint in-game activity; only a validated completion for this binding marks kept, Alex trust +3 once. Merely choosing A has no reward.
B renegotiate before session → propose one available week-four pre-training slot; only explicit mutual confirmation changes the deadline and increments rescheduleCount. Rejection or cancellation of the proposal keeps the original deadline. No immediate penalty.
C cancel before the due slot closes → mark cancelled; Alex acknowledges notice, no trust penalty. An already missed session stays missed and uses S17; cancellation cannot erase it.
Leaving app does not choose C.
If no commitment exists, offer optional joint or individual practice with no accusation.
If player explicitly confirms advancement past a due commitment, apply one missed consequence, trust -3, then offer repair. Slot advancement itself is part of that transaction; cancelling the confirmation leaves the slot open.

S14 difficult assignment / pitch.
Coach offers an evidence-supported task compatible with the player's position.
A accept → supported objective.
B ask why → brief explanation, then accept or retain current task.
C retain current objective → record preference; no automatic respect loss.
If no valid task exists, use ordinary weekly-focus preparation. No forced role swap.

S15 teammate agency / equipment area.
Sam: “Can I try this one on my own first?”
A “Of course.” / B “Want feedback afterwards?” / C “I'll work over there.”
All respect Sam's boundary; vary follow-up invitation. No guaranteed friendship reward.
This scene can replace another optional scene, not exceed the pacing budget.

S16 third-match response / pitch exit.
Use observed evidence to discuss response after a setback or a changed responsibility.
No setback evidence → discuss maintaining concentration or an actual supported event.
A discuss execution / B discuss decision / C choose one next attempt.
Development evaluator remains authoritative. Story records reflection only.

S17 repair / training or home message.
For missed promises only, Alex: “I waited. Next time, just tell me.” For advance cancellation: “Thanks for letting me know. Another time?”
A propose a supported repair session → repair_offered; confirmation binds a new activity run and future slot. No points for offering or apologizing; validated completion after a missed promise restores +2 trust once, below the original -3 loss. Advance-cancelled promises grant no repair points.
B acknowledge impact or explain cancellation → record conversation and close, no points.
C close honestly without another promise → close, no extra penalty.
Unavailable repair activity offers B/C. A second failed repair closes unresolved without another trust loss or another reward opportunity.
No looping apologies for repeated points. If nothing was missed, replace with a quiet non-conflict beat.

S18 preparation / home.
Caregiver responds according to saved advice preference.
Player selects one existing objective linked to the locked week-three focus. This does not change focus; week-four draft focus is edited only by the shared focus service.
Chapter destination and role assignment are shown using real supported scheduling.
Do not award selection for flattering dialogue.

### Week 4 — More Than a Name on the Team Sheet

S19 role explanation / pre-match.
Coach: “{{player.name}}, here's what I'm asking you to do.”
Show actual assigned role and any recorded selection rationale; if no rationale was evaluated, label it a default assignment rather than inventing evidence.
If selection evaluation is unsupported, use a neutral supported assignment and disclose that career selection is not implemented; do not invent a starting-place award.
A accept / B ask a question / C request a supported alternative.
An alternative requires game support and eligibility; otherwise coach explains the current limit.

S20 relationship payoff / touchline.
Alex dialogue selected from commitment state:
kept: “Same movement we practiced?” (only if the bound session practiced that movement; otherwise “Ready to play?”)
repaired: “We sorted it. Let's play.”
cancelled with advance notice: “Another session sometime. Let's play.”
no promise: “I'll show when there's space.”
unresolved: “Let's talk afterwards. We've got a match.”
Everyone still follows normal sensible soccer AI.

S21 chapter match / pitch.
Play existing supported match.
Reserve no extra story interruptions: relevant Coach Moments share the existing 4–6 total.
Record actual events and completion status.
No forced winning goal, forced turnover or hidden physics change.
Pause/resume saves match integration state according to existing game capabilities.

S22 final whistle / pitch exit.
If meaningful supported player event exists, discuss it accurately.
Otherwise discuss recorded participation or ask how the role felt.
Match result remains independent from career interpretation.
A name a learning / B acknowledge teammate / C ask next step.
Save response; do not award technical growth a second time.

S23 chapter outcome / coach conversation.
Select from eligibility rules in section 9.
Explain the opportunity using actual evidence. Keep relationship reactions separate from performance evaluation.
If no supported evaluation exists, use the separate neutral Limited Evidence conclusion, not The Next Chance or a claim of poor performance.

S24 bedroom return / home.
Display chosen name, actual journal, current focus and outcome.
Choose next ambition or retain current ambition.
Save chapter complete once; chapter replay is noncanonical unless starting a separate career.
Show “Next chapter in development” if no authored continuation exists. Do not imply U12+ or tournaments are implemented.

## 9. Outcome resolver

Input: immutable chapter-end snapshot containing careerId, chapterRunId, actorId, evidence IDs, explicit roleExplorationIntent (target role and confirmation occurrence), supported opportunity IDs, and adapter version. Each opportunity evaluation returns eligible | ineligible | unknown, evidence IDs, criteria version, and a player-readable reason. Missing adapters, insufficient evidence, unsupported opportunities or stale versions return unknown, never false/ineligible by default. Adapter thresholds must be mapped to existing tested game criteria and approved tuning before evaluated endings are enabled; no placeholder readiness score is permitted.

Resolve in order:
1. A New Direction: explicit confirmed exploration intent AND eligible supported exploration opportunity.
2. A Place Earned: eligible supported starting opportunity.
3. A Trusted Option: eligible supported contribution opportunity.
4. The Next Chance: at least one applicable opportunity was evaluated ineligible, none is eligible, and none of the applicable evaluations is unknown.
5. Limited Evidence: all other cases, including no supported opportunities or incomplete evaluations. Dialogue: “We haven't got enough information to decide the next opportunity yet. Your chapter is complete; your progress is saved.”

S19 describes the current match assignment only. S23 describes a future opportunity; it may not change a completed match. Persist the resolver result, reasons and snapshot once. Do not reevaluate after reload, renaming or replay. A new chapter creates a new evaluation window. Requesting an alternative in S19 records exploration intent only after an explicit confirmation step; a question alone is not consent to a role change. Match defeat does not exclude any eligible ending. Relationship scores cannot manufacture eligibility. All five conclusions complete the chapter and preserve growth.

## 10. Choice transaction and save integrity

The stable occurrence key is (careerId, chapterRunId, phaseSlotId, sceneSlotId). It identifies a canonical encounter, independent of randomized template, scene-instance UUID, reload or renderer. A choice transaction key adds stepId; an effect entitlement key adds the stable semantic effect purpose. A promise consequence uses commitmentId plus consequence kind; match growth remains keyed and owned by the existing growth system. Never include choiceId or a fresh random instance ID in an entitlement key: alternative submissions must compete for the same entitlement, not create new ones.

Use one IndexedDB readwrite transaction over career state, decisions, event deduplication, entitlements and journal stores. Within that transaction read and compare expected revision, validate active occurrence/step/choice, check evidence and bindings, claim entitlements, write all effects and increment revision. No awaits to network or unrelated asynchronous work inside the transaction. Unique indexes enforce keys. If the existing save system cannot share this transaction, migrate the canonical career into this store before enabling story effects; do not split authoritative state between IndexedDB and localStorage.

Duplicate identical requests return the saved result. A different choice for an already committed step returns a conflict and refreshes the scene. A stale revision returns a retryable conflict with no mutation. Multi-tab BroadcastChannel/locks provide UI coordination only; correctness comes from database transactions and unique constraints. Storage quota, abort or interruption leaves prior state intact and offers retry/export; never show rewards before commit. External matches are consumed through a persisted inbox and stable source-record deduplication. Replaying the same source record under another eventId cannot duplicate effects. Incoming data cannot supply arbitrary effect names or values.

Save every step independently; completion is committed only at its final step. Replay is read-only with effects disabled. Career reset creates a new career identity; it cannot share progression with the old career. Pin active scenes to a content version. Migrations have explicit mappings for occurrences, steps, conflicts and entitlements, validate before committing, and preserve a recoverable prior snapshot. Unknown future saves show recovery UI; never silently reset. Imported saves are size-bounded and schema-validated; reject unknown executable content, prototype keys and nonfinite/out-of-range values. Never merge imported reward ledgers into another career.

Browser-only saves remain editable by their device owner. This does not provide competitive anti-cheat. No backend or leaderboard is introduced.

## 13. Corrected scheduling, steps and focus ownership

This section replaces ambiguous informal sequencing above. A scene number is a content beat, not necessarily a separate encounter. The phase scheduler is authoritative. Every mandatory phase has an explicit skip or neutral continuation and no wall-clock deadline.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S01; initialize focus; S02–S04; optional S05 caregiver beat | concise actual assignment | supported fixture M_W1 or explicit skip | S06 | shared focus service drafts W2; close |
| 2 | lock W2 focus; S07–S10 | actual assignment summary | M_W2 or explicit skip | S11 | S12 reflection component drafts W3; close |
| 3 | lock W3 focus; S13–S15; optional S17 after a missed/cancelled commitment | actual assignment summary | M_W3 or explicit skip | S16 | S18; draft W4; close |
| 4 | lock W4 focus; resolve renegotiated or repair session at its agreed slot | S19 + S20 in one encounter | S21 / M_W4 or explicit skip | S22 + S23 in one encounter | any final commitment closure; S24 |

Each match slot binds exactly one authoritative match ID. Resume returns to that match if supported; otherwise explicitly abandon with no invented result and follow the skipped-match path. A replacement activity cannot silently replace a completed match. Combined pre/post encounters share the 20–45-second target; detailed explanations and evidence views are optional expansions. Story does not add to the existing 4–6 tactical interruption budget.

The weekly-focus service is the sole writer. Week one is chosen before first practice (S05's focus step moves here). At home the player drafts the next week's focus; entering that week locks it for match topic selection. Missing choices carry forward an eligible focus or use a visible role-appropriate default; unavailable catalog entries use neutral preparation. S12/S18 cannot mutate the current locked focus. No rewards for switching focus. Weekly focus affects only eligible tactical topics, never tactical truth.

Every scene definition supplies an ordered step graph with stepId, type, choices, prerequisites, nextStep and terminal flag. Validate no missing destinations and no cycle without an explicit exit. Persist choicesByStep and activeStepId. Required multistep graphs:

- S05 caregiver preference -> optional practice view -> terminal; week-one focus is the separate initialization step above.
- S08 invitation -> slot proposal -> explicit confirmation -> terminal; decline exits immediately.

- S14 task decision -> optional explanation -> accept/retain -> terminal.

## 14. Complete conflict and commitment transitions

Only the following transitions are allowed; all others reject without mutation. Terminal statuses retain their historical facts. Invitations expire harmlessly and never become commitments without explicit confirmation.

| Conflict | Allowed transitions and guards |
|---|---|
| C_ROLE | unseen -> introduced only for supported opportunity; introduced -> competing when eligible challenge/observation starts; competing -> choice when evaluation/discussion is available; choice -> resolved after decision. introduced/competing/choice -> paused records resumeStage; paused -> saved resumeStage only when major slot is free; any nonterminal -> resolved at chapter closure with an honest unresolved/limited-evidence reflection. |
| C_ADAPT | unseen -> requested only for supported task and free major slot; requested -> negotiated after explanation; requested/negotiated -> attempted only on bound task start; attempted -> reflected after completion or explicit abandonment; requested/negotiated -> reflected on retain/decline or unavailable task. At chapter closure every nonterminal -> reflected with actual evidence or neutral wording. |
| C_PROMISE | unseen -> offered; offered -> accepted only after confirmed available slot, or closed on decline/expiry. accepted -> due when the agreed slot opens. accepted/due -> renegotiated only before slot closure with confirmed alternative and rescheduleCount=0; renegotiated -> due at its new slot. accepted/due/renegotiated -> kept only on bound completion, or cancelled on explicit advance cancellation. due -> missed only on confirmed advancement past slot without completion. missed/cancelled -> repair_offered on explicit proposal, or closed after discussion. repair_offered -> repaired only on confirmed bound repair completion; otherwise -> closed on decline, unavailable activity or confirmed advancement past repair slot. kept/repaired/cancelled/missed -> closed after their appropriate final reflection. |

Store outcomeHistory separately from current stage so closed does not erase kept/missed/repaired. Renegotiated remains an accepted obligation. Week-four preparation contains its due slot, with resume, complete, cancel or confirmed advancement choices. Repair proposals must have a supported slot before chapter close; otherwise use honest closure. No endless rescheduling, duplicate apologies or repeat penalties. C_PROMISE occupies the minor thread slot; C_ROLE and C_ADAPT share the major slot. Pausing C_ROLE releases it. A paused conflict can still close neutrally without blocking another thread.

Activities bind careerId, chapterRunId, commitmentId (if any), originOccurrenceKey, initiatingStepId, activityDefinitionVersion and unique activityRunId at launch. Only authoritative completion for that exact binding can satisfy it; prior, replayed, foreign-player or self-reported completions cannot. The binding is consumed atomically with its effect. If joint gameplay is unsupported, do not offer a joint-session promise; offer an unpenalized invitation or solo practice instead. Do not falsely claim Alex participated in a solo activity.

## 15. Evidence lifetime, director liveness and authored consequences

Use current-match evidence for match claims, current-week evidence for weekly feedback and the current chapter/player for the ending. Historical comparisons must explicitly cite their old week and immutable role/team snapshot. Renaming changes display only; it never rewrites historical role, team, decision or evidence ownership.
