## Global scene and assessment contract

This is guided training only: introduce → observe → freeze → user choice → demonstration → critique → varied retry. No free-play match or movement-time gate. Keep completion separate from mastery and reward each scheduled slot at most once.

Roster: each side has GK, LB, CB, RB, 6, 8, LW, RW, 9. The selected protagonist replaces ONE role, including GK when applicable; never add a nineteenth player. Formation numbers include one goalkeeper and eight outfield players. Do not interpret 1-2-4-2 as nine outfield players plus another keeper.

UPDATED USER DIRECTION: source fidelity takes precedence over player count and field coverage. There is no requirement to show all 18 players or constrain every scene to half a field. Use the participants, space, goals and relationships required by the actual source. Keep a readable overhead teaching view of all decision-relevant information; full field, half field or a relevant area is permissible. Do not compress distances or add decorative players. A source exercise can inform a guided tactical situation without becoming a free-play mini-game. An adaptation from 11v11 or a small-sided source to a 9v9 learning context must still be disclosed and reviewed.

Each executable scene must supply every participating player's world-space position, velocity, facing, ball state, possession, attacking direction, actual full-pitch halfway reference where relevant, legal status, available support and cover. Full-team scenes use the roster above; partial scenes use the source's participant structure. These briefs intentionally do not invent exact coordinates or movement thresholds before scene review.

For each brief, the first decision below is preferred ONLY while its stated cue remains true. The alternative is valid under its stated condition. The error is a plausible mistake, not a distractor to display verbatim. Do not use the feedback line as an automatic pre-answer hint.

Suggested scoring design (not scientifically validated): 2 = justified choice for the frozen state; 1 = defensible but lower-value choice with explained tradeoff; 0 = clear contradiction of visible cues; null = ambiguous, missing information, demonstration or unsupported scene. No hidden best-answer tie-breaking. Record assistance separately. Separate execution outcome from decision quality. No mastery from one answer; the mastery threshold is a product decision requiring approval.

Offside: evaluate position at the applicable teammate contact/release, then involvement; position alone is not an offence. Account for own-half eligibility, the ball and second-last opponent, and direct-restart exceptions. Never assume the goalkeeper is always the last opponent. Use local age-group rules for offside, restarts, heading and keeper distribution; no universal U12 rule profile is assumed.

## Goalkeeper

### GK01 — Find the unpressed outlet [S2]
Setup/cue: possession at GK; a striker blocks CB while RB has a clear receiving lane. Decision: distribute to RB with usable receiving space. Alternative: hold briefly or use LB if RB becomes pressed. Error: force the central pass solely because it is shortest. Team response: back line offers angles; midfield separates from markers; opponents shift. Coach: “You used the free outlet, not the nearest shirt.” Counterfactual: move the opponent into the RB lane; the old answer must stop being automatically preferred.

### GK02 — Move to support the return [S2]
Setup/cue: LB is under pressure; GK is hidden behind the presser's line. Decision: adjust to a visible return angle. Alternative: remain if movement would obstruct another safe route. Error: stand still behind the opponent. Team response: CB provides the next outlet; LB decides whether to recycle. Coach: “Your position created an escape before the pass.” Counterfactual: close the return lane and require another support solution.

### GK03 — Reorganize after a loss [S3]
Setup/cue: possession lost centrally, attacker facing goal, GK advanced from build-up. Decision: recover a useful goal-protecting position and communicate. Alternative: intervene if the loose ball is clearly reachable first. Error: rush toward a controlled ball without an interception advantage. Team response: CB delays; wide defenders recover inside. Coach: “Read who could reach the ball before committing.” Counterfactual: lengthen the attacker's touch enough to make intervention viable.

## Centre-back

### CB01 — Cover the defender who steps [S1]
Setup/cue: LB engages a winger; an inside runner threatens behind LB. Decision: give inside/deeper cover while monitoring the runner. Alternative: engage only after responsibility transfers and cover exists. Error: join LB at the ball. Team response: RB narrows; 6 screens the cutback route. Coach: “One pressed; you protected what was behind.” Counterfactual: LB is beaten; CB must now reassess becoming first defender.

### CB02 — Support a coordinated press [S4,S7]
Setup/cue: front line presses a back-facing receiver; midfield follows; CB is detached. Decision: advance with the connected unit while tracking depth threats. Alternative: hold/drop if the ball carrier can release behind or cover is absent. Error: advance regardless of pressure. Team response: back three move together; GK adjusts support. Coach: “Your line supported the press because pressure limited the next pass.” Counterfactual: opponent turns freely; automatic stepping becomes unsafe.

### CB03 — Protect the centre on turnover [S3]
Setup/cue: 8 loses possession; CB faces ball carrier and a separate runner. Decision: delay and protect the central route until support returns. Alternative: intercept a clearly exposed pass. Error: chase wide and open the route to goal. Team response: 6 recovers; outside backs track threats. Coach: “You bought time while keeping the dangerous route closed.” Counterfactual: support arrives; assess whether pressure can increase.

## Left-back

### LB01 — Escape a touchline trap [S5]
Cue: receiving left, forward route blocked, 6 visible inside. Decision: combine inside. Alternative: recycle to CB if 6 is screened. Error: carry into two closing opponents. Response: LW offers depth, 6 opens an angle. Coach: “Your support gave you another direction.” Variation: mark 6 and open the line pass instead.

### LB02 — Decide whether to jump [S4]
Cue: opponent receives left with back turned; CB covers and LW screens return. Decision: press under that support. Alternative: delay if CB is occupied by a runner. Error: leave the line without cover. Response: CB shifts, RB narrows, 6 protects inside. Coach: “The support behind you made that press possible.” Variation: remove cover.

### LB03 — Recover inside first [S3]
Cue: ball lost on the opposite flank; a central runner is more dangerous than a distant wide player. Decision: recover toward the central threat while scanning wide. Alternative: track the wide runner if the central zone is already covered. Error: stay wide by habit. Response: CB communicates assignments. Coach: “Recover toward the danger, not just your starting spot.” Variation: switch the immediate threat wide.

## Right-back

### RB01 — Escape a touchline trap [S5]
Cue: receiving right, line blocked, 6 visible inside. Decision: combine inside. Alternative: recycle to CB if screened. Error: dribble into the trap. Response: RW offers depth; 6 supports. Coach: “You found support before the touchline trapped you.” Variation: mark 6 and open RW. Mirror LB01 spatially, not by swapping text alone.

### RB02 — Decide whether to jump [S4]
Cue: right-side receiver faces backward; CB covers, RW screens return. Decision: press. Alternative: hold if a runner removes CB cover. Error: jump without support. Response: back line and 6 shift. Coach: “Pressure works when the next defender protects you.” Variation: receiver opens toward goal. Mirror LB02 with body orientation preserved.

### RB03 — Recover inside first [S3]
Cue: loss on the left; an uncovered central runner threatens. Decision: narrow and recover to protect that route. Alternative: stay with a wide threat when centre is secure. Error: remain on the touchline regardless. Response: communicate with CB and 6. Coach: “You protected the route the opponent could exploit next.” Variation: central cover arrives. Mirror LB03.

## Defensive midfielder — 6

### DM01 — Become available behind pressure [S5]
Cue: CB has possession; 6 is directly behind an opponent from the ball's perspective. Decision: move laterally into a usable passing angle, scanning before receiving. Alternative: clear the lane for 8 when followed. Error: demand the pass while screened. Response: 8 occupies a different lane. Coach: “A small movement made you visible.” Variation: marker follows tightly.

### DM02 — Cover rather than duplicate pressure [S1]
Cue: 8 presses ball; pass into striker remains open behind. Decision: screen the striker route. Alternative: take over pressure if 8 is beaten and cover transfers. Error: rush next to 8. Response: CB holds depth, wings recover. Coach: “Your job was the next pass, not the same opponent.” Variation: 8 loses the duel.

### DM03 — Reconnect on regain [S3,S5]
Cue: regain facing own goal with immediate pressure and a free supporting CB. Decision: secure possession through support. Alternative: turn forward if scan confirms space. Error: force a blind forward ball. Response: 8 and wings open outlets. Coach: “Winning it did not mean the forward pass was ready.” Variation: remove pressure and reveal a progressive lane.

## Central midfielder — 8

### CM01 — Receive or bounce [S5]
Cue: pass arrives between opponents; pressure is tight behind and a support player is free. Decision: lay off and move. Alternative: turn if pressure has withdrawn and space is confirmed. Error: turn blindly into the marker. Response: 9 occupies a defender; 6 supports at a different angle. Coach: “Your scan decided whether the turn was available.” Variation: withdraw the marker.

### CM02 — Support the front press [S4,S7]
Cue: 9 presses a backward-facing CB; opposing 6 offers the inside escape. Decision: close that receiving lane while staying connected. Alternative: hold deeper if the frontline press is unsupported. Error: chase the same CB as 9. Response: wings close relevant outlets; own 6 covers. Coach: “You helped the press by removing the exit.” Variation: opposing 6 moves to create a different lane.

### CM03 — Choose a supporting lane [S5]
Cue: RW has ball, 9 occupies the direct forward lane, underneath support is missing. Decision: offer a separate backward-diagonal angle. Alternative: run beyond if support already exists and space opens. Error: stand in the striker's lane. Response: 6 balances behind; LW maintains a separate option. Coach: “Different angles gave the winger different choices.” Variation: add underneath support and open depth.

## Left winger

### LW01 — Combine against a second defender [S5]
Cue: receiving left, second opponent closes, LB support remains open. Decision: combine with support. Alternative: beat the first defender if the second is too far to cover. Error: dribble into both automatically. Response: 8 provides another angle; 9 keeps a distinct lane. Coach: “The second defender changed the decision.” Variation: withdraw cover.

### LW02 — Close the outlet in the press [S4]
Cue: 9 pressures CB and opposing RB becomes the available escape. Decision: position to restrict that outlet while retaining team connection. Alternative: recover inside if the press is broken. Error: sprint at the ball and free RB. Response: LB and 8 coordinate behind. Coach: “You pressed the passing option as well as the ball.” Variation: ball bypasses the first line.

### LW03 — Time the run [S6]
Cue: in opponent half, passer preparing release, winger near the second-last-opponent line. Decision: delay/curve to remain legally positioned at the relevant contact. Alternative: check short. Error: leave early and become involved from offside. Response: 9 offers another lane. Coach: “Your starting position mattered when the ball was played.” Variation: defender drops or ball is ahead of winger.

## Right winger

### RW01 — Combine against a second defender [S5]
Cue: on right, second defender closes, RB offers support. Decision: combine. Alternative: attack isolated first defender if cover is absent. Error: force the same dribble despite cover. Response: 8 supports, 9 separates. Coach: “Read the covering defender before committing.” Variation: remove cover. Mirror LW01.

### RW02 — Close the outlet in the press [S4]
Cue: 9 presses CB; opposing LB offers escape. Decision: restrict that outlet in connection with teammates. Alternative: recover inside once bypassed. Error: duplicate 9's pressure. Response: RB and 8 shift behind. Coach: “Your position made their next pass harder.” Variation: bypass the press. Mirror LW02.

### RW03 — Time the run [S6]
Cue: right winger near offside line in opposing half as teammate prepares pass. Decision: time/curve the run from a legal position. Alternative: offer short support. Error: be early and interfere. Response: 9 stays in a separate channel. Coach: “The run and the pass had to work together.” Variation: defender drops; recalculate rather than reuse answer. Mirror LW03.

## Striker — 9

### ST01 — Hold, lay off or turn [S5]
Cue: receiving back to goal with tight pressure and 8 available. Decision: protect and lay off. Alternative: turn if the defender's position and available space genuinely allow it. Error: assume proximity alone makes turning correct. Response: wings offer depth; 8 moves after the layoff. Coach: “You kept the next action available for the team.” Variation: defender backs away and a turning lane opens.

### ST02 — Initiate only a supported press [S4,S7]
Cue: backward reception, winger and 8 ready to close exits. Decision: initiate coordinated pressure. Alternative: screen and wait if teammates are detached. Error: sprint alone and allow an easy bypass. Response: midfield and back line connect rather than remain at goal. Coach: “The trigger mattered because your teammates could support it.” Variation: detach midfield.

### ST03 — Adjust rather than wait offside [S6]
Cue: striker beyond second-last opponent, teammate not yet releasing. Decision: return to an eligible position before the relevant pass contact or offer a different run. Alternative: remain uninvolved while another teammate plays, without interfering. Error: receive after merely stepping back following release and assume legality is restored. Response: 8 waits or chooses another option. Coach: “Coming back after the pass did not erase the earlier position.” Variation: begin in own half; apply the correct rule.
