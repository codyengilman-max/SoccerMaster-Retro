# Research findings and adversarial content audit

## The stronger architecture

Organize a session by its football problem, then let the learner inspect related roles. A player should understand what changes for teammates when one player presses, receives, carries or recovers. Position tags are indexes into those relationships, not independent universal instructions.

The Mississippi Soccer Association index supplies six attacking/defending problem areas, each with two9v9-labelled plans. All25 pages across the12 PDFs were visually inspected, including exercise layouts and final-play sections. Sports Session Planner supplies useful authored examples, but its title alone is not evidence of coherent9v9 roles. Its two included supplements were text-reviewed; their interactive animations were not inspected. These are coaching plans, not controlled evidence of learning effectiveness.

## Role and count safeguards

Product reference roster per team is GK, LB, CB, RB, DM, CM, LW, RW, ST: nine total. DM and CM are the two central midfield slots, not additions to a third midfielder. Boy/girl protagonist choices occupy one of the eight outfield slots. Other valid9v9 shapes require a separately named roster mapping.

Pilots show subsets: P01 nine participants (6home/3away), P02 eleven (5home/6away), P03 seven (5home/2away). These counts are not team sizes. All actors resolve to one of two nine-slot rosters; no anonymous extra neutral appears. Off-camera actors cannot be omitted if they would change the chosen option or its short consequence.

## Evidence boundary

Source URLs and exact sections live in sources.json. Library wording, decision labels, role assignments, geometry, counterfactuals and schedule are original adaptations. They are not copied source answers or approved by the named source authors. This is an internal source/logic review, not an independent licensed coach sign-off.

Source material was selected for relevance to9v9/U11–U12, explicit player relationships and usable coaching questions. Other investigated SSP buildout, wide-play and midfield examples were not admitted to the core pack because they added mixed shapes, neutral-player constraints or unnecessary duplication. We did not use elite11v11 tactics as direct evidence for these new scenes.

## Release gates still open

1. A qualified youth coach reviews each rendered state and every accepted/rejected option, signing a versioned approval record.
2. Confirm the project's competition rules and pitch profile; current dimensions are a declared draft, not a universal9v9 regulation.
3. Measure animation reach, ball interception, spacing and contact timing. Ensure text cues and actual sprites agree.
4. Check all18 roster identities even when only subsets render; evaluate omitted threats and transition protection.
5. Verify both protagonist sheets, persistent outline, phone readability, rotation, pause/retry/save behavior and accessible non-color team cues.
6. Bind approved content to the existing progression engine only after the weekly scale has been reviewed separately.

The structural checks included here do not close these gates. No source is represented as commercially licensed for copying; the package contains links and original descriptions only.
