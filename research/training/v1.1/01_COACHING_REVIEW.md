# Coaching review — 15 October 2026

## Verdict

Suitable as a draft technical-foundation catalog and software reference. NOT ready for final animation production or player publication. This is an AI-assisted coaching-content review informed by primary coaching resources, not independent certification by a licensed manager. Reviewed all 100 written variant sequences, family cues, space profiles and publication policy. No source videos or completed animations were inspected. No children or live training sessions were observed.

The prior ten audit fixes address specification/security concerns. They do not close the coaching issues below. The 63 reference tests were rerun successfully during packaging, but they do not test movement correctness.

## Findings and required revisions

| Priority | Affected content | Finding | Required correction / acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | PA02, RC01, JU01–JU03 | “Foot”, “settle” and “gentle touch” leave contact mechanics unspecified. | For each demonstrated variant specify contact surface, support stance, ball arrival height/phase, contact action and exit. Choose one teachable technique per demo; do not silently mix cushioning, blocking and upward juggling. Reviewer must identify the exact action from the frames without captions. |
| High | TR01, TR02, TR03 | Pull-back direction, cut angle and Cruyff exit are underspecified for animation. TR02's sideways redirect is not necessarily an outside-hook reversal. TR03-L1 is an isolated component, not a complete deceptive turn. | Label components as foundations. Define starting orientation, working/support foot, ball route relative to support leg, body rotation and exit for both sides. Keep multiple valid variations distinct. The Cruyff's inside-foot redirection behind the support leg is supported; a selected source's outside-foot exit is a variation to inspect, not a universal rule. |
| High | FI02 | Firm ankle/laces alone is insufficient to prescribe a grounded-ball strike. Toe clearance and support-foot setup are missing. | Add a reviewed balanced setup, top-of-foot contact presentation, comfortable ankle/toe position and safe swing clearance. Do not prescribe toe scraping, maximal effort, mandatory airborne follow-through or a universal landing rule. Check the actual demo from side and oblique views. |
| High | PA01, FI01 | Instructions state inside-foot contact but provide little assistance for a child who cannot produce the intended ground trajectory. | Specify a comfortable support-foot position beside the ball, balanced posture, stable inside-foot surface, central contact for the chosen ground pass and controlled follow-through. Use approximate individualized cues, not rigid joint angles or fixed plant-foot distances. Show a common error and correction. |
| Medium | All variants | Generic starting/ending fields say ball may be held or caught even for ground-only exercises. | Resolve start/end text per variant. Ground drills must begin on the floor and end with controlled stopping; aerial variants must specify the release, bounce/contact count and catch/reset. No generic template should contradict the demo. |
| Medium | RC01, RC02, PA02 | First-touch instructions prioritize stopping and resetting; lateral receiving lacks a defined body/foot relationship to the next action. | Keep stop-and-control beginner options. Add explicitly reviewed receive-to-next-action options; show preparation before return and a reachable directed touch. Do not require opening the body or using the back foot in every situation. RC02 wall service remains an original adaptation of a two-player drill. |
| Medium | DR03, RC01-L4, FI01-L5 | Preselected exits/targets offer rehearsal but little genuine information-dependent choice. | Label these as route checking or preplanned technique. Do not claim they prove scanning under pressure or tactical awareness. Later responsive-cue solo tasks need separate design and review, and still cannot reproduce an opponent. Never encourage looking at a handheld screen while dribbling. |
| Medium | Whole catalog | Many options are introductory, with substantial duplication. JU01-L1 and JU02-L1 repeat the same sequence; CO01-L1 repeats the BM01 foundation. PA01 and FI01 overlap heavily. | Keep stable IDs for compatibility, but label shared foundations and reuse reviewed assets. Do not market 100 variations as 100 distinct skills. Separate familiarization, repeatability and connected-action challenges according to learner readiness. No mandatory repetition quota or weaker-foot-as-higher-level claim. |
| Medium | DR01, DR02, TR01–TR03 | Repeated walk/stop instructions are useful initially but insufficient as the entire progression for experienced U11–U12 players. | Preserve beginner options and develop reviewed continuous-control and exit changes in suitable space. Add one challenge at a time. Do not increase pace simply to fill levels or push beyond the space profile. |
| Medium | Catalog coverage | No dedicated feint family beyond Cruyff disguise; no shielding, defensive-footwork or goalkeeper curriculum. | Describe this release as selected outfield technical foundations. Expand deliberately later with sourced solo-appropriate families. Do not add token defending drills or claim solo tasks teach tackle timing/pressure reading. Goalkeeper work is a separate gap, not implicitly covered. |
| Medium | Learning outcomes | Quality cues mostly concern basic control; match-use connections are thin. | Add a brief purpose and limitation to each family: for example receiving across the body may prepare an exit when space allows. Tie to game situations without awarding real-world tactical mastery or promising automatic transfer. |

## What is already sound

Solo eligibility, genuine equipment checks, optional participation, manageable practice, no heading/contact-tackle prescription, no need for filming children, no automatic tactical-mastery claim, both-foot opportunities, accurate mirroring semantics and draft publication gates are appropriate design choices. The catalog correctly treats five options as variations rather than a proven difficulty ladder. Preserve these safeguards.
